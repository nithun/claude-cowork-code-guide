    Product playbook — idea → BR / PRD

    For product folks. You mostly live in Cowork (the planning half). Your job is to turn ideas and problems into the named documents engineering builds from. You don't need to touch code.

    Pick the right document

    Three situations, three entry agents. Getting this right saves a rewrite:

    You have… Entry agent Output Say
    A new capability ("students should get a certificate") prd-author docs/prds/PRD-NNN-*.md "Write a PRD for …"
    A non-trivial bug ("re-engagement fires after opt-out") prd-author (bug mode) docs/bug-reports/BR-NNN-*.md "Write a bug report for …"
    A change to how an existing feature works ("drop students at end of grace instead of pausing") change-requirement docs/change-requests/CR-NNN-*.md "Write a CR for …"

    Rule of thumb: new thing → PRD. Broken thing → BR. Different thing → CR. prd-author auto-picks PRD vs. BR by scope; you don't have to.

    The flow

    (optional) brainstorm in Cowork
      ↓
"Write a PRD for X"   →  prd-author asks you clarifying questions
      ↓
PRD-NNN lands in docs/prds/   ←  you read it, correct it, approve it
      ↓
"Decompose this into tasks"   →  task-manager → docs/.../*-tasks.md
      ↓
(handoff to engineering — see the Engineering playbook)

    1. Brainstorm first (optional but recommended)

    Before committing to a doc, think out loud with Claude in Cowork — pressure-test the problem, who it's for, what success looks like, what you're not doing. Cowork ships product brainstorming skills you can lean on. A good brainstorm gives prd-author sharper raw material and means fewer clarifying rounds.

    You don't have to brainstorm — if the idea is clear, go straight to "Write a PRD for …".

    2. Let prd-author interview you

    prd-author will probe: the problem, the user, the goal, scope (and non-goals), success metrics, acceptance criteria. Answer plainly. It will also flag if your idea actually raises an architecture question and pull in architecture-reviewer — that's a feature, not a delay; it means a real decision gets recorded as an ADR before code is written.

    3. What a good PRD/BR looks like

    You're the approver, so know the bar:

    • Problem & why now — the pain, not the solution.
    • User & goal — who, and what they can do after.
    • Scope and non-goals — non-goals are the most valuable lines in the doc; they prevent scope creep.
    • Success metrics — how you'll know it worked.
    • Acceptance criteria — concrete, testable statements. Engineering turns each into a test.
    • (BR) Repro steps + expected vs. actual.

    If a section is hand-wavy, send it back before approving. A vague PRD becomes a vague feature.

    4. Approve, then hand off

    When the PRD reads right, say "Decompose this into tasks" — task-manager breaks it into a backlog and writes a tasks file. That file is the handoff to engineering. (Task lists don't cross the Cowork↔Code boundary; the file does.)

    For a behavior change, the gate is different: you flip the CR's Status: Proposed → Approved. After approval the CR body is immutable — further changes are append-only. That immutability is what lets feature-builder trust it as the source of truth.

    Worked example

    Idea: "Students should get a certificate when they finish a program."

    1. (Cowork) Brainstorm: who downloads it, what's on it, what about partial completion? Decide certificates are completion-only (non-goal: partial/streak certs, v1).
    2. (Cowork) "Write a PRD for certificate-on-completion." prd-author asks: trigger condition? format (PDF/image)? who can re-download? You answer.
    3. PRD-007 lands in docs/prds/. You read it — the non-goals capture the v1 boundary. You approve.
    4. "Decompose this into tasks." task-manager writes the tasks file: certificate DocType, generation on completion, download endpoint, tests.
    5. Engineering picks it up → Engineering playbook.

    Your phrasebook

    • "Brainstorm a feature for …" — think it through first
    • "Write a PRD for …" — new capability → prd-author
    • "Write a bug report for …" — non-trivial bug → prd-author (BR mode)
    • "Write a CR for …" — change an existing behavior → change-requirement
    • "Does our architecture support …" — sanity-check feasibility → architecture-reviewer
    • "Decompose this into tasks" — ready to hand off → task-manager
